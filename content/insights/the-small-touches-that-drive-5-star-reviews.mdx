# The Small Touches That Drive 5-Star Reviews

## Guests remember how effortless and considered the stay felt.

Five-star reviews are rarely earned by one expensive amenity. They are built through small signals: the entrance is easy to find, the room feels prepared, the water is cold, the charger is where it is needed, and help arrives before frustration becomes a complaint.

These touches work because they communicate care. The goal is not to fill the property with gifts. It is to remove friction at the moments guests notice most.

> **The most valuable small touches solve a real guest need before it becomes a request.**

## Get the fundamentals right first

No welcome basket can compensate for weak cleaning, an uncomfortable bed, unreliable Wi-Fi, or confusing access. Delight sits on top of trust.

The foundation is:

- Immaculate, consistent cleanliness
- Accurate listing expectations
- A comfortable sleep environment
- Reliable essentials and working amenities
- Clear arrival and departure instructions
- Fast, calm service recovery

Only after these standards are dependable should you invest in extras.

## Focus on five high-memory moments

![Five High-Memory Moments](/images/journal/the-small-touches-that-drive-5-star-reviews/review-moments.svg)

### 1. The approach

Guests form an impression before they enter. Make the house number visible, illuminate the path, remove trip hazards, and ensure the door and lock feel maintained. A simple arrival photo can prevent a stressful start.

### 2. The first five minutes

The property should feel intentionally reset: comfortable temperature, fresh neutral scent, a lamp on when appropriate, clean surfaces, and a visible starting point for Wi-Fi and house essentials.

### 3. The first night

Sleep shapes the review. Provide supportive pillows, quality sheets, effective window coverings, bedside power, and a quiet-room solution where location allows. Place extra linens where guests can find them without asking.

### 4. The first problem

The response matters more than perfection. Acknowledge the issue, give a specific next step, own the follow-through, and confirm resolution. Guests often reward excellent recovery because it proves the host is attentive.

### 5. The final morning

Avoid ending the stay with a punitive chore list. Make checkout clear, short, and gracious. A thoughtful departure message should thank the guest and remove uncertainty—not pressure them for a rating.

## Choose touches with operational staying power

The best touches are repeatable across turnovers. Consider:

- Chilled, filtered water or a clean refill station
- Clearly labeled coffee and tea choices
- A universal charging cable or accessible outlets
- Makeup towels and luggage racks
- Child, pet, or work essentials when the listing serves those guests
- A locally relevant snack with ingredient information
- Umbrellas, hooks, and a drop zone where climate makes them useful
- A short, genuinely curated neighborhood list

Avoid touches that create sanitation concerns, difficult inventory, allergy risk, or inconsistent expectations.

## Personalize with context—not surveillance

Use information the guest volunteered. If they mention a birthday, family trip, pet, or late arrival, a small relevant adjustment can feel deeply personal. Do not overstate what you know or make guests feel monitored.

A note saying “We hope you enjoy the concert” can be warmer than an expensive generic basket. Relevance creates the value.

## Turn compliments into standards

Read reviews for specific language. When guests repeatedly mention the easy check-in, comfortable mattress, stocked kitchen, or thoughtful recommendations, you have identified a repeatable experience driver.

![Touchpoint Priority Matrix](/images/journal/the-small-touches-that-drive-5-star-reviews/touchpoint-matrix.svg)

Prioritize touches that are:

- Frequently noticed
- Meaningful to the target guest
- Low-risk to deliver consistently
- Easy to inspect during turnover
- Affordable across the property’s economics

Remove touches that are rarely noticed, often missing, hard to clean, or costly without improving sentiment.

## How Luxe Haven Thinks About Guest Delight

Traditional hospitality asks:

> What can we give the guest?

Hospitality Performance Management asks:

> Where does the guest experience uncertainty or friction, and what repeatable touch can resolve it?

This keeps delight connected to operations and outcomes. The touch is not valuable because it looks generous. It is valuable because it improves the experience reliably.

## Operator Checklist

- Walk the arrival path after dark.
- Test the bed, Wi-Fi, shower, and bedside charging.
- Review the first five minutes from a new guest’s perspective.
- Stock only touches the turnover team can verify.
- Track guest mentions by touchpoint.
- Keep ingredient and allergy information with consumables.
- Replace gifts with service recovery when a core promise fails.
- Reassess the program each season.

## Key Takeaways

- Reliability creates the foundation for delight.
- Focus on high-memory moments, not decorative clutter.
- Relevant touches outperform expensive generic gifts.
- Service recovery can become a five-star moment.
- Turn repeated compliments into operating standards.
